Why the Ethiopian calendar has thirteen months

Twelve equal months create a calm, predictable year. Pagume, the small thirteenth month, keeps the calendar aligned with the solar cycle.

12 × 30days in the main months
5 or 6days in Pagume
mod 4 = 3Ethiopian leap-year rule

A simple way to picture it

Begin with Meskerem and count through Nehase: every month has thirty days. Then add Pagume before the next Enkutatash. In most years Pagume has five days; before an Ethiopian leap year it has six. This is why converting by subtracting a fixed number of days is unreliable.

Use a real date conversion

A reliable converter turns both dates into Julian Day Numbers, then converts back. That preserves Pagume, leap days and month boundaries instead of approximating them. For planning, always record which calendar a date uses and include the year explicitly.

Why Fasika and related fasts move each year

Unlike fixed Ethiopian dates such as Genna and Timket, the Orthodox Paschal cycle depends on a calculated lunar relationship.

Start with Nineveh

Bahire Hasab calculations locate the Fast of Nineveh. From that anchor, related observances follow in sequence: Great Lent begins fourteen days later, Hosanna arrives near the end of Lent, and Siklet and Fasika complete Holy Week. Ascension and Pentecost continue the cycle afterward.

Plan with the right level of certainty

Fixed dates and calculated Orthodox dates answer different questions from estimated Islamic dates. Moon sighting and official announcements can shift Islamic observances. A useful planner should show that distinction rather than presenting every date as equally certain.

Everyday time · 3 min read

How to read Ethiopian traditional time without confusion

The traditional twelve-hour cycle begins around dawn and again around dusk. The clock face may look familiar, but the reference point changes.

In everyday terms, 1 o’clock in the morning corresponds to about 7:00 AM, while 1 o’clock in the evening corresponds to about 7:00 PM. Add six hours to the traditional daytime reading, or subtract six from the modern reading. Always keep AM/PM and the time zone in the conversation.

Ethiopia uses East Africa Time, UTC+3, throughout the year. Traditional time changes the spoken reading, not the underlying time zone.

Creator craft · 4 min read

Make Amharic short-video captions easier to read

Good captions are edited for reading speed, contrast and screen position — not merely copied from a script.

Keep each idea together

Use one or two short lines and avoid separating a verb from the phrase it completes. Let viewers finish reading before the caption changes. For vertical video, keep important words away from the interface controls at the right and the description area near the bottom.

Test on a phone, not just the editor

Preview at normal phone size, check contrast over the brightest footage, and listen once without looking to see whether the words match the spoken rhythm. Consistent placement beats adding a new font or animation to every sentence.
