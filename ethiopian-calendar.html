About the Ethiopian calendar

How does the 13-month system work?

The Ethiopian calendar has 12 months of 30 days each, plus a 13th month (Pagume) of 5 days — 6 in a leap year. Leap years occur when the year number modulo 4 equals 3.

Why is the Ethiopian year different?

The Ethiopian year count follows a different era (roughly 7–8 years behind the Gregorian calendar), and the new year (Enkutatash) falls on September 11 — or September 12 after a Gregorian leap day.
